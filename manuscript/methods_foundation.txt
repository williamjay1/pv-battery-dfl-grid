2. Methods

2.1. Data, information and evaluation units

The Ausgrid Solar Home Electricity Data contain half-hourly electricity measurements for 300 PV households between July 2010 and June 2013 [L30]. We retain general consumption (GC), gross PV generation (GG) and separately metered controlled load (CL). Published interval kWh are multiplied by two to obtain average kW. The battery offsets GC minus GG. CL is added to feeder demand and settled at the same assumed AUD 0.18/kWh in all methods; it cannot generate a paired battery advantage. A household without any observed CL circuit is treated as having no such circuit, whereas intermittent CL gaps remain missing. Estimated observations and missing required channels are excluded from clean evaluation, without imputation.

Dates and 48 daily intervals follow the published source clock. We do not assign an unverified UTC or daylight-saving correction. The predictor uses only measurements before the target day, calendar information and the last available nominal PV capacity. No future realized load, generation or weather enters deployable predictions. Training is July 2010–December 2011, validation January–June 2012 and testing July 2012–June 2013. Customer 2 is excluded under the established missing-record rule. Identifier hashing allocates 244 known households and 55 household-held-out households. The latter are excluded from model fitting but provide their own past measurements at deployment.

Private economics and feeder consequences use different evaluation units. The expanded private comparison retains every paired usable household-day among the 299 included households. Absolute operating cost and retail bill additionally require a valid CL observation; GC/GG-only cost differences remain estimable when the same CL charge cancels. The feeder analysis requires all assigned households to have clean observations on a date. Three disjoint 55-household panels have 219, 129 and 276 common clean test dates; the 55-household held-out panel has 274. Monthly coverage and load/PV characteristics of retained dates are reported explicitly. Neither block resampling nor expansion of private coverage corrects selection of the feeder-complete dates.

2.2. Household control and cost accounting

Each simulated battery has 10 kWh capacity, 3 kW charge and discharge limits, one-way efficiency 0.95 and an energy range of 1–9 kWh. The reference day-ahead controller uses half-hour steps and initial and terminal energy of 5 kWh. This cyclic constraint allows plans to be concatenated physically while excluding interday inventory choice. Charging from the grid is permitted. The reference throughput price κ is AUD 0.02/kWh. It is a linear operating-cost proxy, not an observed battery-degradation law. Reported realized operating cost replaces n_hat with actual GC minus GG, adds CL settlement and excludes the artificial selection term. The pure retail bill and battery throughput are reported separately. Capital expenditure, fixed service fees and life extension are outside the estimated outcomes.

The time-of-use purchase prices are AUD 0.18/kWh at 00:00–07:00 and 22:00–24:00, AUD 0.50/kWh at 16:00–21:00 and AUD 0.30/kWh otherwise. The flat price is AUD 0.30/kWh; both pay AUD 0.08/kWh for exports. These are stated study tariffs, rather than reconstructed contracts of the historical households. A no-battery reference and perfect-information optimization provide scale. Perfect information is privileged and its cost gap is not assumed to be entirely learnable. A self-consumption rule is a secondary reference with a different continuous-state policy.

The import epigraph gives a convex quadratic program. Sparse Clarabel solves it [L34]. The ε=10⁻⁴ AUD selection term is strictly convex in battery actions and state, so objective comparisons use a reproducible action-selection rule instead of arbitrary choices among economic optima. Gradients of the selected solution are calculated from its active-constraint KKT system. Implementation checks, finite-difference diagnostics, alternative ε values and the equal-buy/sell negative control are reported in the Supplementary Methods. These numerical checks establish consistency of the stated model, not physical accuracy of an unobserved residential feeder.

2.3. Statistical and decision-focused training

The common PV network has 774 historical and calendar inputs, ReLU hidden layers of 128 and 64 units, and 48 softplus outputs. Demand histories are divided by a fixed 10 kW scale; PV histories and targets use capacity observed before the target day. Its initial MSE training uses 132,200 known-household training days and selects a configuration on 44,373 validation days. Seeds 11, 23 and 47 use that selected configuration. A shared histogram gradient-boosted demand model supplies the reference load forecast.

The reference decision-focused comparison initializes from the corresponding MSE checkpoint, uses the same fixed 4,096 training household-days, four epochs, batches of 32, AdamW regularization and two candidate learning rates, and selects by realized cost on 1,024 fixed validation household-days. Continued MSE receives the same data, update budget, optimizer family, learning-rate candidates and cost-based validation selection. The initial checkpoint remains eligible. A separately retained exact-learning-rate control and the frozen MSE model diagnose update and selection effects; continued MSE is the primary attribution comparator. Actual training consumption and generation enter the realized cost C_i as targets, not as prediction-time inputs. At deployment, A_ε sees the load forecast minus the learned signal. An adjustment to the latter can therefore be algebraically equivalent to a net-load correction. We call it a controller task signal and do not interpret reduced operating cost as improved physical PV information.

An initial test diagnostic identified positive task signals in conservatively dark slots. A fixed restriction to zero at 00:00–04:00 and 21:00–24:00 was consequently inserted in training, validation and deployment, and matched models were retrained. The original test period was retained. This redesign and its initial results remain disclosed; the revised Ausgrid test is a computational case study informed by diagnostics, not an untouched confirmation sample. True meter observations are unchanged. The mask is neither a complete irradiance model nor a verified installed-capacity ceiling. Real generation occasionally exceeds published nominal capacity, so we do not mechanically clip measurements to that field.

2.4. Practical alternatives and resource measurement

A three-parameter controller-cost calibration adds bounded capacity-normalized offsets at 06:00–10:00, 10:00–14:00 and 14:00–18:00. A seasonal extension allocates the same three windows to four calendar seasons, giving 12 parameters. Both fit only training examples and choose shrinkage only on validation data. They test whether a shared low-complexity signal correction captures the decision benefit; they do not deploy the across-household correction calculated from test-set signal decomposition.

A direct net-load MLP predicts GC minus GG with unrestricted signed output and is compared before and after controller-cost calibration. This tests whether placing the correction in a separately named PV output is necessary. For the reference demand forecaster, persistence and a full-training-data load model supply deployable input changes. Frozen-signal transport is separated from newly matched MSE/DFL training under each input. True-load substitution is retained only as a privileged exploratory diagnostic.

The residual-scenario controller minimizes expected cost across 20 joint daily load/PV residual paths selected deterministically from validation records. Each scenario shares the same planned battery actions; no scenario-specific perfect-information recourse is allowed. Residual-day pairing preserves within-household load/PV association. Unseen households use established training-household donors. Because optimizations are separable by household, this is not a network-coordinated or cross-household correlation-aware controller. Methods have common pre-day information but differ in their uncertainty representation, a legitimate distinction for engineering choice.

Training and scheduling times are measured in isolated, single-thread processes on the same fixed sample counts. Peak resident memory and environment are retained. Cache reuse during expansion of already verified dispatch does not count as an algorithmic speed improvement. Update and maintenance requirements are compared alongside measured cost and feeder outcomes; differing training sample sizes are stated rather than presented as matched training-budget effects.

2.5. Feeder conditions and voltage interpretation

The principal network is the public European low-voltage benchmark [L23], with 55 single-phase locations, published impedances, a 21/19/15 phase allocation and an unchanged 800 kVA transformer. Historical households are assigned to locations; their true feeder connections are unknown. Gross load has power factor 0.95, and PV and batteries have unity power factor. Unbalanced constant-power AC replay is performed in OpenDSS. Additional SimBench rural and semiurban systems use their 160 and 400 kVA transformers and balanced pandapower replay [L19,L20]. Native generation and storage are disabled before household injection.

Continued MSE is the primary comparator across source voltages 1.000, 1.025 and 1.050 pu, the two alternative topologies and ten fixed mappings. Frozen MSE remains a secondary operational reference. Changing only the feeder model does not retrain household signals. Limited power-factor and impedance brackets test physical-assumption sensitivity. Published-network reference checks and numerical power balance are distinguished from model-to-field validation.

We separate samples above 1.05 pu and below 0.95 pu, report voltage quantiles and worst locations, and distinguish mean excursion over all customer half-hours from magnitude conditional on exceeding a limit. Conditional magnitude is undefined when there is no exceedance. Consecutive exceedance duration is computed within valid contiguous observations. Complete fixed upper- and lower-threshold curves are reported, including the wider 0.90–1.10 pu range; thresholds are not selected for favorable effects. These are screening criteria, not reconstructed legal compliance tests. Relative import/export peaks, losses, transformer loading, nominal PV-to-transformer ratio and battery-to-daily-load ratio make the engineering scale explicit.

Main deployment cells contain 28 time-of-use battery owners, 55 time-of-use owners or 55 owners with 28 time-of-use and 27 flat-price tariffs, on each of three panels and three seeds. A separate held-out panel tests household portability. A 27-household postcode cohort is compared with 27 dispersed households selected from pretest load and PV capacity, with matching roles, the same electrical locations and the same clean dates. It checks a constructed cohort sensitivity and cannot identify the effect of geographic concentration or recover actual neighborhood wiring.

2.6. Uncertainty and claim scope

Primary contrasts average paired daily differences across three seeds before calendar-block resampling. New fixed-model engineering comparisons use seed 11 and explicitly retain that narrower training-replication scope. Confidence intervals use 2,000 circular moving-block resamples with seven-day blocks on the complete calendar axis and retain missing masks; established primary checks also use 14- and 28-day blocks [L29]. Dates are synchronized across methods. Household half-hours and seeds are not treated as independent observations. Private expanded contrasts preserve their household-day denominators, with household-average distributions reported separately. The intervals describe temporal stability conditional on records and models, not national population representativeness or uncertainty in feeder construction. Physical sensitivities, worst cases and household harm tails are therefore reported directly rather than absorbed into one confidence interval.
